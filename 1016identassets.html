Module 3 · Identifying Key Assets

What Are Key Assets — and How Do We Find Them?

Not all critical infrastructure carries the same weight. Key assets are a distinct subset of infrastructure whose disruption or destruction would produce catastrophic consequences — measured in lives, economic loss, or national confidence.

This module examines the three major categories of key assets, the federal framework for identifying and prioritizing them, and the ongoing challenges of information sharing and regulatory compliance that shape how protection decisions get made.

Part 3 · Document Highlights

Protecting Key Assets

Source: National Strategy for the Physical Protection of Critical Infrastructures and Key Assets
▸ Three Categories of Key Assets

The National Strategy identifies three distinct categories: (1) national monuments, symbols, and icons that represent heritage and political power; (2) facilities representing national economic power and technological advancement — many housing hazardous materials; and (3) mass gathering locations such as commercial centers and sports stadiums.

▸ The Tourism and Media Factor

National monuments and symbols typically attract large amounts of tourism and frequent media attention — factors that impose additional protection challenges by increasing both visibility and the potential impact of an attack on public morale.

▸ Dual Stakes: Lives and Confidence

Given the national fame of key asset sites and the potential human consequences of an attack, protecting them is important in two distinct ways: preventing fatalities and preserving public confidence in the government's ability to provide security.

▸ Hazardous Materials at Economic Facilities

A critical feature of economic and technology facilities is that many house significant amounts of hazardous materials, fuels, and chemical catalysts. Disruption does not just harm the facility — it can cascade into major public health and safety consequences across entire regions.

Part 4 · Document Highlights

Identification, Risk Management & Policy

Sources: NIPP Supplemental Tool — Risk Management Approach & CRS Report RL30153
▸ The NCIPP National Inventory

The National Critical Infrastructure Prioritization Program builds and maintains a national inventory of assets deemed significant at federal, state, and local levels — based on hazards/threats, vulnerabilities, and potential cascading consequences to other infrastructure.

▸ The Five-Step Risk Management Framework

The NIPP framework guides partners through five iterative steps: Set Goals and Objectives → Identify Infrastructure → Assess and Analyze Risks → Implement Risk Management Activities → Measure Effectiveness. Physical, cyber, and human elements must be considered throughout.

▸ Information Sharing: A Two-Way Challenge

The federal government wants private sector data on vulnerabilities and incidents; the private sector wants government threat data. Each side protects its information — the government for classification reasons, industry for liability and reputation concerns — creating persistent friction in the sharing process.

▸ Regulation Varies Dramatically by Sector

The regulatory landscape is uneven: nuclear plants face very specific, enforced vulnerability standards, while ports must submit security plans under the Maritime Transportation Security Act. But sectors like information technology, oil and gas, and commercial real estate face only voluntary guidelines — not mandates.
