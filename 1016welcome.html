A Quick Hello Before We Begin

"Hey everyone… glad to have you here. My name is Tom Barberio and I'll be your professor for this course. Before I ever set foot in a classroom, I worked as a Computer Crimes Detective in New York City. There, my investigations had a strong emphasis on computer and mobile forensics, as well as child exploitation investigations. Towards the end of my career, I was responsible for the planning of large-scale events and protest response in the city. I'm currently partners in a computer forensics investigative firm, with locations in New York and Florida."

This course looks at how the assets that keep a society running actually get protected — how they're identified, assessed, and defended, and what happens when they fail. We'll pull straight from current events and real-world case studies to make it concrete.

Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course, you'll be able to:

  • Identify the processes and techniques used to sustain critical infrastructure partnerships.
  • Explain the value of those partnerships to infrastructure protection and resilience.
  • Prepare and evaluate after-action reports.
  • Apply the techniques covered in class to current events and relevant crime trends.
  • Explore advancements in technology as they relate to securing critical assets.

Part 3 · How This Course Works

A New Briefing, Week by Week

Each week, you'll open a new sector briefing — a realistic scenario built around that week's concepts, whether it's a regional power outage, a compromised water system, or a disrupted supply chain. You'll review the key terms, work through a short field-notes account drawn from real situations, and then work the briefing yourself.

This is a fully remote class run through videos, presentations, articles, and discussion — and it leans heavily on current events and real case studies to keep things grounded.

Initial discussion posts are due by 11:59 PM Thursday; peer responses are due by 11:59 PM Sunday. My advice every semester: post early in the week so you've got a buffer if something comes up.
